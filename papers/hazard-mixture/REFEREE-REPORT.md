# Independent referee report: hazard-mixture manuscript

Manuscript: "Majorization does not order the hazard rates of exponential mixtures with more than two components" (`papers/hazard-mixture/main.tex`, branch `paper/hazard-mixture`, commit 7bbec25, dated 25 September 2026).

Referee: an independent AI referee (Claude, model Fable 5.1) acting adversarially on 25 September 2026. The referee took no part in writing the manuscript or its scripts. Every mathematical claim below was re-derived in a fresh SymPy session (`referee_check.py`, written before the authors' `verify/` scripts were opened); the sources were fetched and read by the referee; `REVIEW-NOTES.md` was read last.

## Verdict

Accept after minor revision. No mathematical error was found.

- Theorems 3.1, 4.1, 5.1, 5.4, Corollaries 5.2 and 5.5, and Propositions 6.1, 6.2 and 7.4 re-derive exactly (identities, rational values, majorizations, T-transforms, root counts, limits, means and variances).
- The inequalities point in the direction that contradicts the cited statements: for t < t_* the hazards are ordered as the published theorems predict, for t > t_* the order is reversed.
- The quotations from Shojaee, Asadi and Finkelstein (2022) and from Sahoo, Kayal and Finkelstein (2026) are verbatim, the statement numbers are right, the order conventions (Definition 2.1(ii): Y <=_hr Z iff h_Y >= h_Z) are the ones the manuscript uses, and no hypothesis of the cited statements excludes the examples (h = 1 > 0, alpha = 1 >= 0, theta = 1; uniform or strictly antiordered weights lie in V_n; the intermediate matrix of instance (4) lies in V_3).
- The verify suite runs with the specified interpreter, exits 0 with 85 passes, contains no floating-point comparison, and the four script hashes match Section 9.

The required changes concern presentation and attribution (items 1 to 4), one consequence the note omits that would strengthen it (item 5), and housekeeping (items 6 to 13). None affects the main result.

## Problems

Severity: blocking (must change before submission), accuracy (a stated fact is wrong or misleading), minor (presentation), suggestion (optional but recommended).

1. **Blocking. Author placeholder.** `\author{[Owner name to be confirmed]...}` (main.tex line 28). Fix: insert the author name and affiliation; keep the `\thanks` AI-disclosure footnote and check its wording against the target journal's policy on AI-assisted manuscripts.

2. **Accuracy. Wrong name for the root-isolation algorithm.** Section 9 (line 468) says real-root isolation is "by the Vincent-Collins-Akritas method", and the docstring of `verify_two_crossings.py` (line 11) says the same. The scripts call SymPy `Poly.intervals`, which implements the Vincent-Akritas-Strzebonski continued-fraction algorithm with LMQ positive-root bounds (`sympy/polys/rootisolation.py`, `dup_isolate_real_roots`; SymPy cites Akritas and Strzebonski 2005 and Akritas, Strzebonski and Vigklas 2008), not the Collins-Akritas bisection method. Both are Descartes-rule methods and the certification is equally sound, but the attribution is wrong. Fix: "real-root isolation by the Vincent-Akritas-Strzebonski continued-fraction method as implemented in SymPy's `Poly.intervals`", citing Akritas and Strzebonski (2005) or keeping Basu, Pollack and Roy Chapter 10 for Descartes-based isolation in general. If the script docstring is corrected too, recompute the SHA-256 on line 478; otherwise leave the script untouched and fix only the text. (`count_roots` is indeed Sturm-sequence based, as stated.)

3. **Accuracy. "Degree 275" is the reduced degree.** The proof of Proposition 6.2 and Section 9 (line 477) speak of "the degree-275 numerator". Formed directly, the numerator 40(N_lambda D_gamma' - N_gamma' D_lambda) of h_lambda - h_gamma' in s = e^{-t/40} has degree 279; 275 is the degree after SymPy's `cancel` removes the factor (1 + s + s^2)^2 common to numerator and denominator (referee: gcd = (s^2 + s + 1)^2). Fix: "after cancelling the common factor (1+s+s^2)^2 the numerator has degree 275", so that a reader forming the difference by hand is not misled.

4. **Accuracy (minor). Rounded digits behind an ellipsis.** Theorem 3.1(c) prints t_* = 0.823057445625...; the expansion is t_* = 0.82305744562489256910..., so the twelfth decimal is rounded while the ellipsis suggests truncation (q_* = 0.43908711514625... is fine). The authors' own log prints t_* in (0.823057445625, 0.823057445627) from `sp.N(x, 12)`, which rounds. Fix: write t_* = 0.8230574456249... or "t_* ~ 0.823057445625" without the ellipsis. Table 1 uses six rounded decimals without ellipses, which is fine.

5. **Suggestion (strengthens the note; one paragraph in Section 7.2).** The same instances refute the reversed-hazard-rate statements of Sahoo, Kayal and Finkelstein: Theorem 3.11 (page 23) and Corollary 3.3, which are proved by the same Lemma 2.5 step, and Theorem 3.12 for products with different structures (whose proof is again deferred to Hazra and Finkelstein 2018, Theorem 3.4). Two facts, both checked by the referee:
   - (a) In the proofs of Theorems 3.10 and 3.11 the reversed hazard rate of U_n is displayed as theta^{-1} g(t/theta) sum_i p_i gamma_i G^{alpha gamma_i - 1}(t/theta) / sum_i p_i G^{alpha gamma_i}(t/theta) = theta^{-1} r(t/theta) htilde_{p,gamma}(G(t/theta)^alpha), where htilde_{p,gamma}(y) = sum_i p_i gamma_i y^{gamma_i} / sum_i p_i y^{gamma_i} is exactly the exponential-mixture hazard rate in the variable y = e^{-t}. Under that formula the comparison in Theorem 3.11 is literally the comparison in Theorem 3.8 and fails at the same points (htilde_{(1,3,5)} - htilde_{(1,4,4)} changes sign at q_*). Note that this displayed formula is the reversed hazard rate of the alpha-mixture of the distribution functions G^{gamma_i}(t/theta), not of the survival model (1.3); the authors may point out this inconsistency, with care and after checking the version of record.
   - (b) Under the model (1.3) as printed (alpha-mixture of survival functions), the reversed hazard rate of the exponential mixture is r(q) = sum_i p_i gamma_i q^{gamma_i} / (1 - sum_i p_i q^{gamma_i}), q = e^{-t}. For instance (1), r_{(1,3,5)} - r_{(1,4,4)} has numerator -q^3 (2q^4 + 4q^3 + 4q^2 + 8q - 9), exactly one root in (0,1), and is positive at q = 1/2 and negative at q = 9/10. Theorem 3.11 asserts U_n <=_rh V_n, that is r_{p,gamma} <= r_{q,delta} (Definition 2.1(iii)), for V_n and alpha >= 0; the value at q = 1/2 contradicts it. So Theorem 3.11 fails under either reading of the model.
   - Also worth a sentence: Theorems 3.8 and 3.11 together would give U_n >=_hr V_n and U_n <=_rh V_n, hence U_n >=_st V_n and U_n <=_st V_n, which forces U_n and V_n to have the same distribution; this is further evidence that the n-component step, not the two-component lemma, is at fault.
   - Two scope sentences would also help. First, the failure of Theorem 3.8 is independent of the baseline, of theta and of alpha > 0: for the model (1.3), h_{U_n}(t) = theta^{-1} h(t/theta) htilde_{p,gamma}(Gbar(t/theta)^alpha), and y = Gbar(t/theta)^alpha runs through (0,1) as t runs through (0, infinity), so every positive baseline hazard, every theta and every alpha > 0 inherit the sign change of the same rational function. Second, the alpha <= 0 (W_n) half is not touched by the examples: there y = Gbar^alpha > 1, and with y = 1/q the difference htilde_{(1,3,5)}(1/q) - htilde_{(1,4,4)}(1/q) has numerator 2(q-1)(q^4 - 2q^3 - q - 1), which has no root in (0,1) and is positive there, the direction the theorem predicts. The manuscript should say explicitly that it makes no claim about the alpha <= 0 half.

## Priority, novelty and tone

- The mathematics is elementary and the example is small; the contribution is to settle the extension asked for in SAF Remark 6.3 in the negative for every n >= 3, to locate the exact gap in SKF (Lemma 2.5 applied to a functional that is not additive over columns), and to show that no condition on the weights or the rates alone can rescue the statement (Remark 5.6, Proposition 6.1). The referee found nothing earlier that states this. The mechanism (Theorem 5.1) and the criterion (Theorem 5.4) are modest but correct and are what make the note more than a numerical observation.
- The two-component remark correctly records that n = 2 (SKF Theorem 3.7) is not contradicted, and the title's "more than two components" is justified by Theorem 4.1 (all n >= 3, uniform weights) and Proposition 6.1 (strict weights).
- Tone toward the cited authors is factual and appropriate. The referee suggests, as is customary for a correction note, that the authors inform the SKF authors before submission.
